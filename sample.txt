Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that improves large language model answers by retrieving relevant documents at query time and adding them to the prompt.

A typical RAG pipeline has four steps. First, documents are loaded and split into small overlapping chunks. Second, each chunk is converted into a vector embedding. Third, the embeddings are stored in a vector database such as ChromaDB or FAISS. Fourth, at question time the most similar chunks are retrieved and passed to the language model as context.

RAG reduces hallucinations because the model answers from supplied text instead of relying only on its training data. It also lets a system use private or recent documents without retraining the model.

Chunk size matters: chunks that are too large dilute relevance, while chunks that are too small lose context. A common starting point is 500 to 1000 characters with 10 to 20 percent overlap.
